# Reference analysis — Steel Ball

Scope: the 20 clips in `videos/best` (about 22 minutes). Every clip was sampled into
timestamped contact sheets (one frame every 0.5–3 s), then the key moments were pulled
at full resolution, and the audio of the spin/throw moments was analysed as
spectrograms. Timestamps below are `clip-id @ seconds`.

Two limits to state up front:

- **I cannot listen to audio.** Everything in the sound section comes from spectral
  measurement, not from hearing it. Pitch, modulation rate and timing are measured;
  timbre ("does it sound right") is not something I can judge.
- **No clip contains isolated sound effects.** All audio is a full mix (music,
  dialogue, effects), and four of the clips are music edits.

## A. Appearance

**Colour.** Not steel-grey. In every anime shot the ball is a light yellow-green
(pistachio / lime), roughly `#9fcf5a` in daylight, with dark forest-green in the
grooves and near-white highlight patches. The opening renders it as a more saturated
glossy lime metal (`6UF_qprQiu0 @ 81.2`, `@ 87.5`). At night it is graded toward
blue-green (`xgQQpKhIeCo @ 29.8`).

**Engraving.** The design is *not* a soccer ball, although it reads that way at a
glance. Stationary shots (`xgQQpKhIeCo @ 29.8–30.3`, `mVSUucEP-pU @ 5.3–8.4`,
`6UF_qprQiu0 @ 81.2`) and all three Sketchfab models agree on:

- a hexagonal groove around each pole (a "cap"),
- slots running from the hexagon's corners down the body toward the equator, ending
  in rounded tips before they meet,
- grooves that are wide recessed channels with a lighter bevel on one edge, not thin
  lines. In close-up the channel floor is darker green and the lit bevel is nearly
  white.

**Shading.** Cel-shaded with two or three tones plus hard-edged specular patches, one
large and soft-cornered, toward the upper left. The opening adds a bright rim and
mirror-like reflections. The real machined prop (`-6uO8l_YU3Y`) shows what the metal
actually does: one broad hot highlight, a darker lower half, and the room reflected.

**How it shows spin.** This is the most distinctive thing in the references:

- The surface turns into streaks that run *around* the spin axis: jagged, saw-toothed
  bands of pale green and near-white with short dark-green dashes
  (`3GACRm9WXFc @ 20.4–28.3`, `feiPAzvOcf0 @ 6.8–8.0`). The engraving is no longer
  readable, only its smeared remains.
- Two to four thin white/lavender elliptical rings orbit the ball like planetary
  rings, perpendicular to the spin axis, slightly tilted, redrawn every frame.
- The silhouette wobbles between round and slightly egg-shaped, frame to frame.
- The real props confirm the physics: at speed the pattern collapses into smooth
  latitude bands around the axis and a stable highlight streak
  (`CBtSkJ6H4Hg @ 6.8–10.8`, `-6uO8l_YU3Y @ 4.2–7.2`). Engraving reappears as the
  ball slows (`CBtSkJ6H4Hg @ 11.2`).

**Under acceleration.** Streaks get longer and whiter, rings get larger and more
numerous, and a yellow-gold glow wraps the ball (`xgQQpKhIeCo @ 12.3–13.5`).

## C. Sound

Measured, not heard (see the limits above).

- **The Spin has a signature tone.** A narrow, steady tone at **847–848 Hz** is
  present in every scene where a ball is spinning, across four different clips, and
  absent in a control passage with no ball (`WhrRNqDzias @ 2–11`). It starts on the
  exact frame the ball activates (`WhrRNqDzias @ 14.5`) and holds for as long as the
  ball spins. Identical pitch in unrelated scenes means it is a stock effect layer,
  not music.
- The tone is **pulsed** at roughly **3.5 Hz** (amplitude modulation, deep), and
  sits 5–9 dB under the full mix. Weaker companion partials appear at about 376, 493
  and 625 Hz depending on the scene.
- **Activation** (`WhrRNqDzias @ 14.5`): a wide noise burst from about 500 Hz to
  16 kHz that decays over ~1.5 s, with a fast downward sweep (≈500 → 50 Hz in 0.1 s)
  underneath, i.e. a thump.
- **Throw** (`xgQQpKhIeCo @ 11.0–11.8`): a noise band at 1–3 kHz with several
  upward-sweeping components (≈300 → 1000 Hz over 0.4 s), i.e. a rising whoosh.
- **Could not determine:** the dry character of any single effect, because nothing
  is isolated. I did not cut samples out of the clips for this reason: any cut would
  carry music and dialogue with it.

Decision: the app synthesises its sound with the Web Audio API from these
measurements (848 Hz tone, 3.5 Hz pulse, companion partials, noise bursts, rising
sweep), and will play your own files instead if you drop them into `assets/audio`
(names are listed in the README).

## D. Composition and grading

- **Colour shift on activation.** The whole frame is pushed toward saturated
  green/teal with purple shadows, and skin goes magenta-purple
  (`WhrRNqDzias @ 14.8–19.8`). During a throw the palette flips to red/orange with
  gold (`@ 109–115`). At night, cyan and blue.
- **Light from the ball.** A soft green-gold bloom behind the hand, strongest at the
  palm, and a rim light on the fingers facing the ball.
- **Speed lines.** Radial white/pale-blue streaks converging on the ball, on a
  darkened background (`xgQQpKhIeCo @ 13.2`), or diagonal streaks along the throw.
- **Circular energy.** Concentric distortion rings and swirls around the ball, drawn
  as refraction with a rainbow fringe (`WhrRNqDzias @ 17.2`, `@ 114.4`).
- **Chromatic aberration.** Visible on ring edges and in the release shockwave
  (`feiPAzvOcf0 @ 8.8`), brief, not a constant filter.
- **Impact.** Star flare, white-out for a frame or two, lightning-like gold sparks.

For a webcam the full-frame palette swap would destroy the picture, so the app keeps
it local: the grade is strongest near the palm and fades with distance, and it only
peaks during materialisation and the throw.

## What this means for the build

1. A 3D ball with the hexagon-and-slot engraving, lime-green metal, and real
   rotational motion blur computed around the spin axis.
2. An anime layer on top of the physical render: saw-toothed streak bands, orbiting
   rings, silhouette wobble, all stepped at 12 fps.
3. Materialisation built from the activation beat: bloom behind the hand, gold lens
   shimmer, flash, then the ball spinning up from a readable engraving into streaks.
4. Throw as a true 3D flight toward the lens with a gold comet tail, speed lines,
   and a refracting shockwave on exit.
5. Sound built around the measured 848 Hz pulsed tone.
